beOI contest & registration

Who is allowed to take part?

The contest is open to pupils enrolled in primary or secondary education during the first semester of the school year of the contest (so from September to December {{schoolYearStart}} for beOI’{{contestYearShort}}). Participants must either hold Belgian nationality or be enrolled in a school established in Belgium. Candidates must also be under 20 years old on 30 June of the year in which the contest takes place (for example for beOI’{{contestYearShort}}, the participant must be born on 1 July {{birthCutoffYear}} or later). Organisers may ask for proof of age and of enrolment in the school.

What are the age categories?

The contest has four age categories. Each participant must register in the category that matches their year of study:

  • Primary — primary school.
  • Cadet — 1st–2nd year of secondary school.
  • Junior — 3rd–4th year of secondary school.
  • Senior — 5th, 6th and 7th year of secondary school.
How much does registration cost?

Participation in the contest itself is entirely free. For optional trainings we offer, a contribution toward lodging and meals may be requested to cover part of those costs.

I live abroad — can I take part?

Belgians living abroad or enrolled in a secondary school abroad may take part in the Belgian Olympiad.

I am not Belgian — can I take part?

If you are not Belgian but enrolled in a Belgian school, you may register for the Belgian Olympiad in Informatics. If you are not Belgian and not enrolled in a Belgian school, you cannot take part in our olympiad. Most countries run their own contest; many are listed on ioinformatics.org.

Can I take part if I am on exchange abroad during the contest?

We handle this case by case. Remote qualification may be possible if we have a reliable contact point (school or university). Please contact us at info@be-oi.be in any case.

Can I take part both at my school and at a regional centre?

No — that would be treated as an attempt to cheat.

For schools

Under what conditions can my school host the qualification round?

The qualification can be organised at your school if all of the following are met (rules may be adjusted slightly when registration opens):

  • A computer room with one computer per participant and an Internet connection.
  • Constant supervision for the full duration of the round.
  • The round takes place during the contest week (start between 8:30 and 15:30).
Do my pupils need to register on your website?

No. Pupils are registered by the school.

How do I register my school?

School registration is done via the contest platform.

Is a computer room required?

The qualification is held on computers, so it must take place in computer rooms with Internet access.

Is a good Internet connection required?

No — the contest is very lightweight and can practically run with an intermittent connection.

Is special software needed on the computers?

No — only a web browser is required.

What if my computer room is too small?

You can run the round in several groups without any problem (explained on the coordinators’ platform).

Do I have to register each pupil one by one?

No — you receive a code per sitting group and pupils enter their own names (explained on the coordinators’ platform).

Until when can I create groups?

There is no hard limit — you can create them two minutes before the round if you wish, but it will be harder to help you if something goes wrong.

Qualification rounds

Where do the qualification rounds take place?

Qualifications take place in schools (see below) and in regional centres. Exact addresses are published when the contest approaches.

Can I sit the French-language contest in a Dutch-speaking centre (or the reverse)?

You can sit the contest in your language in any Belgian centre. Supervisors at centres in the other language community may not always be able to answer you in your language. Questions during the round only cover practical details (time remaining, …), not the tasks themselves.

Can I take the qualification at my school?

Yes, if a teacher is willing to host it and the school meets the conditions described under “For schools”. Otherwise, go to the nearest regional centre.

Final and beyond

Where does the final take place?

The final takes place at a central venue for participants, typically in Brussels. The exact location is announced on our site once it is known.

Do I need to know how to program for the final?

Programming is part of the later stages of the contest. Finalists are expected to solve algorithmic problems in common languages such as Python or C++. When resources allow, we organise trainings between rounds; dates and venues are announced when known.

What if I already program well?

We invite you to join our competitive programming pathway (beCP trainings and contests). It prepares talented programmers for advanced algorithm courses and international competitions such as the International Olympiad in Informatics. See Competitive programming for how to get involved.

How can I take part in the International Olympiad?

Belgium sends a team of four students to the International Olympiad in Informatics each year. The team is selected through the national pathway (several contests and training over multiple years) among the most advanced contestants. Start by progressing through beOI and competitive-programming trainings.
